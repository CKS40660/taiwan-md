> **30-Second Overview**
>
> Tsai Ming-kai founded MediaTek in 1997 with a small team spun off from UMC, starting with CD-ROM chips. In 2004, the company entered the mobile market, feeding the entire Shenzhen knockoff phone ecosystem with "turnkey solutions" — earning the "king of knockoff phones" label that stuck. But by 2024, MediaTek's global mobile chip market share reached 39%, nearly 1.5x that of longtime rival Qualcomm (25.7%). Annual revenue surpassed US$19 billion, with Apple, Google, and NVIDIA lining up to collaborate. This company once dismissed as "only making cheap goods" is now the world's fifth-largest IC design firm.

## A Boxing Fan and His IC Empire

During his years at Kaohsiung Senior High School, Tsai Ming-kai became obsessed with amateur boxing matches. He drew a lesson from the ring: few boxers can reign continuously, because once you stop evolving, the next challenger will knock you down. He later turned this observation into a management philosophy called the "One-Generation Champion" theory: the IC design industry is forever a case of new waves pushing out the old; a single product line is a dead end.

(Source: [NTHU Alumni Interview](https://y105.site.nthu.edu.tw/p/406-1094-92120,r5328.php?Lang=zh-tw) and [TechNews](https://technews.tw/?p=629553))

In 1983, Robert Tsao recruited the 33-year-old Tsai Ming-kai from ITRI to UMC to lead the multimedia group. In 1997, as UMC pivoted strategy, it spun off its IC design division; Tsai led the team to establish MediaTek. The company chose the English name "MediaTek" — media technology — because its founding products were CD-ROM and DVD player chips. No one imagined then that this small Hsinchu company would challenge Qualcomm two decades later.

> 📝 **The "One-Generation Champion" theory explains why MediaTek keeps switching tracks: CD-ROM → mobile → 5G → AI → ASIC. Not because it's fickle, but because Tsai Ming-kai believes staying put equals waiting to die.**

## The Two Faces of the Knockoff Phone King

In 2004, Tsai Ming-kai decided to pivot from optical disc chips into the mobile market. He dispatched his top lieutenant Hsu Chih-chiang to knock on Nokia and Motorola's doors. Hsu poured cold water on the idea: "Unless we have unique technology and are the only option, those big players won't even give us the time of day, and there's no margin." (Source: [Mirror Media 2025 Cover Story](https://www.mirrormedia.mg/story/20241231fin001))

Tsai listened. He went with the flow, abandoning the big-brand courtship and instead entering through China's Shenzhen knockoff phone market. MediaTek's 2.5G chips adopted a "turnkey" model — integrating processor, memory controller, RF components, and software into a single platform. Shenzhen's small factories could take the chips and assemble phones, shipping within months.

This strategy triggered an explosion in China's knockoff phone industry. MediaTek's market share once surged to 40%. But the "king of knockoff phones" label came with it. A MediaTek veteran recalled: "Knockoff phones are imitations after all; the reputation after making big money wasn't good, and Chairman Tsai also felt the knockoff title wasn't very glorious." (Source: [Mirror Media](https://www.mirrormedia.mg/story/20241231fin001))

> 📝 **This is the most interesting paradox in MediaTek's story: the business model that earned them their first pot of gold was precisely the label they'd later fight desperately to shed. The knockoff phone king's success was both starting point and curse.**

## Hitting Rock Bottom: 11% Market Share

When the iPhone debuted in 2007, the smartphone era arrived and knockoff phones faced extinction. MediaTek, technically weaker than Qualcomm, was always a step behind on flagship chips — late to 3G, late to 4G, losing benchmark scores. Qualcomm built a moat with its patent licensing model, poaching brand customers one by one.

At its worst, MediaTek's mobile chip market share plummeted to **11%**.

Tsai Ming-kai made two decisions. First, stop going head-to-head with Qualcomm in the high end; instead pursue "technology for the masses" — packing premium features into mid-range chips at lower cost. The 2014 Helio series let mid-priced phones run octa-core processors, winning massive adoption from Xiaomi, Meizu, and OPPO. Second, in 2017 he brought in former TSMC CEO Tsai Li-hsing as co-CEO, leveraging his big-firm connections and process management experience.

## Dimensity Counterattack: From Chaser to Leader

In 2020, MediaTek launched the "Dimensity" (天璣) 5G chip series. This time it wasn't eating Qualcomm's leftovers — the Dimensity 1000 series was among the industry's first integrated 5G SoCs, with benchmarks going toe-to-toe with Qualcomm's flagships. That Q3, MediaTek's mobile chip market share surpassed Qualcomm's, reclaiming the top spot.

The late-2024 Dimensity 9400 was a decisive blow. Built on TSMC's 3nm process with significantly upgraded AI performance, Chinese top-tier brands scrambled for it — flagship chip shipments surged 70%. Counterpoint Research data shows MediaTek captured 39% of the global smartphone processor market, while Qualcomm fell to 25.7%. Flagship chip sales grew 350% from 2022 to 2025 ([Techsponential](https://www.techsponential.com/reports/dimensity9500)).

Huawei's US sanctions and cutoff from custom chips inadvertently gave MediaTek a boost — Chinese phone brands had no alternative but to rely even more heavily on this Taiwan company that could secure TSMC's advanced processes.

## Eating Apple: Eight Years to Sharpen One Sword

Tsai Ming-kai wasn't content with only Chinese customers. Eight years ago, he formed a dedicated team led by Deputy General Manager Hsu Hao-chun with a single goal: break into Apple's supply chain.

In December 2024, Bloomberg reported that the next-gen Apple Watch would adopt MediaTek modem chips, replacing Intel ([Bloomberg / TechNode](https://technode.com/2024/12/12/mediatek-beats-intel-to-supply-modem-chips-for-apple-watch/)). Three days later, a Morgan Stanley report named MediaTek as winner of Google's custom TPU chip order. Add the NVIDIA × MediaTek AI PC chip collaboration announced by Jensen Huang himself at Computex 2024 — of the world's top four tech companies by market cap (Apple, Google, NVIDIA, Samsung), MediaTek landed three in one go.

An insider told Mirror Media: "Chairman Tsai's been in a great mood lately, because the Apple Watch project took eight years and finally bore fruit."

> 📝 **The logic of MediaTek's entry into Apple mirrors its knockoff market playbook — don't assault the flagship phone chip stronghold (that's Qualcomm's turf); instead enter through the side door of Apple Watch. Get Apple used to your chips first, then negotiate the next step. Tsai Ming-kai's patience: eight years as proof.**

## Walking the Geopolitical Tightrope

MediaTek's greatest risk hides inside its greatest strength: over half its revenue comes from Chinese customers. As the US-China tech war escalates, MediaTek walks a tightrope — biggest market on one side, critical technology sources (US EDA tools, TSMC advanced processes) on the other.

Tsai Li-hsing's response strategy is "don't put all eggs in one basket": aggressively expand non-Chinese customers (Samsung, Apple, Google) while growing North American investment. But geopolitical variables aren't controllable by any corporation. Trump 2.0 tariff policies, China's unwritten "de-Americanization" rules, cross-Strait tensions — any could rewrite the rules.

Morgan Stanley analysts are oddly optimistic: "MediaTek is one of the few Taiwan tech players that benefits on both sides of the US-China divide. Chinese brands need it for TSMC capacity; US giants need it to lower costs." ([Mirror Media 2025](https://www.mirrormedia.mg/story/20241231fin001))

## Not Just Phones: The Next Fight

Tsai Ming-kai's "One-Generation Champion" theory forces MediaTek to keep finding the next ring. Current bets are on three fronts:

**AI Edge Computing** — Since the 2018 Helio P90, MediaTek has embedded dedicated AI Processing Units (APU) in its chips. As on-device AI becomes standard, this early investment is paying off. The Dimensity 9400's on-device generative AI capability is the key reason Chinese brands are scrambling for it.

**ASIC Custom Chips** — Google's TPUs, NVIDIA's AI accelerators — cloud giants increasingly want to design their own chips. MediaTek entered ASIC design services; though later than Broadcom and Marvell, it offers faster execution and better cost control. TrendForce's latest report suggests MediaTek has likely secured Google TPU v7e and v8e orders ([TrendForce, 2026/03](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/03/20/news-csps-accelerate-asic-push-in-2h26-challenging-nvidia-as-mediatek-guc-alchip-benefit/)).

**WiFi and Connectivity** — MediaTek is the world's largest WiFi chip supplier, from home routers to enterprise gear. In the WiFi 7 era, this advantage only grows. When every device must connect, connectivity chips are the admission ticket.

Tsai Ming-kai is 75 this year. He has taken MediaTek from CD-ROM to mobile, from knockoff to flagship, from China to Apple. Over the past six years he poured NT$500B into R&D, building a research army that's 81.8% master's and PhD holders. His "One-Generation Champion" theory describes others — but in the end, he himself is the boxer who refuses to be knocked out.

In early 2025, Tsai Ming-kai walked into TSMC's 2nm process customer list. Beside him stood Apple, NVIDIA, AMD, Qualcomm. Twenty-eight years after that small UMC spin-off, the company has reached the same ring.
