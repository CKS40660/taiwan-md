> **30-Second Overview**
>
> Formosa Plastics Group is Taiwan's largest petrochemical group, founded in 1954 by Wang Yung-ching (王永慶), known as "the God of Management." The group includes four listed companies—the "Formosa Four Treasures" (Formosa Plastics, Nan Ya Plastics, Formosa Chemicals & Fibre, and Formosa Petrochemical)—with over 100,000 employees and combined annual revenues of approximately NT$3 trillion. Starting from a small PVC factory, it has developed into an industrial empire spanning petrochemicals, plastics, fibers, and electronic materials, serving as a major driver of Taiwan's industrialization.

## Why It Matters

The importance of Formosa Plastics Group extends far beyond its revenue figures. It is a vital symbol of Taiwan's transition from an agrarian society to an industrial one and a key component of the "Taiwan Miracle." In an era lacking capital, technology, and talent, Wang Yung-ching built this petrochemical empire through a spirit of "diligence and simplicity."

The group created the "Formosa Management Model," which influenced the entire Taiwanese industrial sector. Its management philosophy—characterized by meticulous cost control, vertical integration strategies, and talent cultivation systems—has been studied and imitated by countless Taiwanese enterprises.

In today's era of ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) and net-zero transitions, how Formosa Plastics Group transforms from a traditional petrochemical giant into a green industry player will serve as a critical indicator for the transformation of Taiwan's manufacturing sector.

## Corporate Overview

Formosa Plastics Group was co-founded in 1954 by brothers Wang Yung-ching and Wang Yung-jai (王永在). With petrochemicals at its core, the group has developed an upstream-to-downstream industrial chain covering crude oil refining, petrochemical raw materials, plastic processing, fiber textiles, and electronic materials.

The group is best known for its "Formosa Four Treasures": Formosa Plastics (台塑, 1301), Nan Ya Plastics (南亞, 1303), Formosa Chemicals & Fibre (台化, 1326), and Formosa Petrochemical (台塑化, 6505). These four listed companies have achieved a vertically integrated business model ranging from oil refining to finished products.

The group's core competitiveness lies in economies of scale and vertical integration. By controlling the entire industrial chain, Formosa Plastics can reduce costs, improve efficiency, and react quickly to market changes. This "one-stop" operating model allows the group to maintain an advantage in the highly competitive petrochemical industry.

## Key Facts (By the Numbers)

The scale of Formosa Plastics Group reflects the results of its half-century vertical integration strategy. Combined revenues of NT$3 trillion, 100,000 employees, and production bases spanning four continents represent an industrial chain that started with PVC powder and extended upstream to crude oil refining.

The divergent performance of the "Four Treasures" in 2025—where Formosa Petrochemical and Nan Ya turned profits while Formosa Chemicals & Fibre and Formosa Plastics faced losses—demonstrates how fluctuations in single petroleum derivative prices can impact the group's overall finances. This is also the direct incentive behind the group's push for its "Five Major Transformations."

The combined market capitalization of the Four Treasures exceeds NT$1.5 trillion, forming the backbone of the energy and materials sectors in the Taiwan stock market; their fluctuations often drive the direction of the overall market index.

**Group Scale**:

- Combined Revenue: Approximately NT$3 trillion (2025 total for the Four Treasures)
- Total Employees: Over 100,000 (including overseas locations)
- Production Bases: Taiwan, Mainland China, USA, Vietnam, and other locations
- Total Assets: Over NT$2.5 trillion

**Performance of the Four Treasures** (2025):

- Formosa Petrochemical: Revenue of approx. NT$1.5 trillion; leading the group in profits
- Nan Ya: Revenue of approx. NT$650 billion; achieved counter-cyclical profit growth
- Formosa Chemicals & Fibre: Swung from profit to loss due to raw material costs
- Formosa Plastics: Facing challenges with increasing loss burdens

**Industry Status**:

- Formosa Petrochemical is Taiwan's largest private refinery and holds the top petrochemical capacity in Taiwan
- Nan Ya possesses global competitiveness in electronic materials
- Formosa Chemicals & Fibre is Taiwan's largest chemical fiber producer
- The group as a whole ranks first among Taiwanese private enterprises in terms of revenue scale[^4]

- Bonuses are calculated based on the overall profit performance of the group
- This reflects the corporate culture of sharing operational results with employees

## Development History: From Rice Shop Apprentice to Industrial Empire

**Founding Period (1954–1970)**:
In 1954, Wang Yung-ching and several founding partners, including his brother Wang Yung-jai, co-founded "Taiwan Plastics Industrial Co., Ltd." (initially operating under the name "Fuhua Plastics Industrial Co.")[^1] to produce PVC (polyvinyl chloride) powder. Initial funding involved US aid loans; records regarding the exact investment structure vary, and it was not a simple NT$500,000 private investment. This marked the starting point of Formosa Plastics Group and the dawn of Taiwan's petrochemical industry.

The early years were fraught with difficulty. PVC was a new product with low market acceptance; technology relied entirely on imports from Japan, leading to high learning costs; more critically, the Taiwanese market was so small that a single factory's annual output could exceed total national demand.

Wang Yung-ching's solution was "vertical integration": since selling PVC powder alone was not profitable, he decided to process it into plastic products. In 1958, Nan Ya Plastics was established to produce daily necessities such as plastic leather, raincoats, and slippers, pioneering a "produce and sell" business model.

**Expansion Period (1970–1990)**:
During the economic takeoff of Taiwan in the 1970s, demand for petrochemical products grew rapidly. Formosa Plastics seized the opportunity to expand capacity quickly. In 1973, Formosa Chemicals & Fibre was established to enter the textile raw materials market. In the 1980s, the group expanded into electronic materials, providing critical components for the development of Taiwan's technology industry.

During this period, the group established the famous "Mailiao Industrial Park." Facing land constraints, Wang Yung-ching chose to reclaim land from the sea in Mailiao Township, Yunlin County, to build a large-scale integrated petrochemical complex. This decision was criticized as "madness" at the time but later proved to be a highly visionary strategic layout.

**Peak Period (1990–2010)**:
With the establishment of Formosa Petrochemical in 1992, the group officially entered the refining industry, and the structure of the "Four Treasures" was finalized. The Mailiao Sixth Naphtha Cracker began operations in 1998[^5], granting Formosa Plastics a complete industrial chain from crude oil to finished products.

This was the golden age of Formosa Plastics Group. Leveraging economies of scale and vertical integration, the group secured a significant position in the global petrochemical industry. Consequently, Wang Yung-ching was revered as "the God of Management," and the Formosa management model became a classic case study for business schools.

In October 2008, Wang Yung-ching passed away at the age of 92[^2]. He dedicated his life to Formosa Plastics, deeply imprinting the "Formosa Spirit" into the corporate culture.

**Transformation Period (2010–Present)**:
Following Wang Yung-ching's death, the group entered the post-Wang era, led by a collective "Nine-Person Decision-Making Group." The new leadership team faces the challenge of finding new directions for the traditional petrochemical industry amidst rising environmental awareness and net-zero requirements.

The divergent performance of the Four Treasures in 2025—with Formosa Petrochemical and Nan Ya growing profits while Formosa Chemicals & Fibre and Formosa Plastics faced losses—indicates that the group is under pressure to adjust its industrial structure. The group has announced a push toward "Five Major Transformations": low-carbon, energy, healthcare, battery materials, and electronic products, hoping to find growth momentum in a new era.

## Global Influence: A Benchmark of the Petrochemical Industry

**Model of Supply Chain Integration**:
Formosa Plastics' vertical integration model is regarded as a benchmark by the global petrochemical industry. From crude oil imports to final product sales, the group controls the entire value chain, a model that significantly increases efficiency and risk resistance.

The Mailiao Sixth Naphtha Cracker complex, in particular, is considered one of the most mature petrochemical industrial parks in the world. Within this park, crude oil can be processed through various stages into diverse chemicals and plastic products, drastically reducing logistics costs and time.

**Exporter of Management Philosophy**:
Formosa's management philosophy has influenced manufacturing across Asia. The corporate spirit of "diligence and simplicity, seeking the root of matters, and striving for excellence," along with its meticulous cost-control systems, has been adopted by countless enterprises.

The concept of "Management Rationalization" proposed by Wang Yung-ching emphasized managing companies through data and systems rather than relying on personal experience. This scientific management approach played a vital role in raising the overall standard of Taiwan's manufacturing industry.

**Driver of Regional Economic Development**:
Formosa Plastics' investments have driven the development of Yunlin County. Mailiao has transformed from a remote seaside town into an industrial powerhouse for Yunlin. The group also invests in plants in Vietnam, the USA, and elsewhere, creating local jobs and driving economic growth.

However, Formosa Plastics also faces environmental controversies. Air pollution issues in the Mailiao Industrial Park and the impact on local fisheries are social responsibility issues that the company must address.

## Challenges and Outlook

**Net-Zero Transition Pressure**:
The global trend toward net-zero emissions places immense pressure on the petrochemical industry. Formosa Plastics must invest in clean production technologies, improve energy efficiency, and even pivot toward bio-based chemicals. While these transition investments may affect profits in the short term, they are essential for long-term survival.

**Raw Material Cost Volatility**:
The petrochemical industry is highly sensitive to crude oil prices; the 2025 losses at Formosa Chemicals & Fibre were linked to rising raw material costs. The group needs to use financial hedging tools or adjust its product structure to mitigate these risks.

**Stricter Environmental Regulations**:
Environmental regulations are tightening globally, with emission standards for the petrochemical industry increasing annually. Formosa Plastics must invest in environmental protection equipment and develop greener products and processes.

**Intensifying Market Competition**:
The Middle East and the US shale oil revolution have brought new competitors, while the petrochemical industries in emerging Asian markets are developing rapidly. Formosa Plastics must enhance its competitiveness to maintain its advantage.

**Future Outlook**:
In response to these challenges, Formosa Plastics Group has proposed a "Five Major Transformations" strategy:

1. **Low-Carbon Transformation**: Developing carbon capture technology and producing bio-plastics to reduce carbon emissions.
2. **Energy Transformation**: Investing in renewable energy and developing energy storage technologies.
3. **Healthcare**: Utilizing chemical expertise to enter the medical materials field.
4. **Battery Materials**: Providing key materials for the electric vehicle revolution.
5. **Electronic Products**: Expanding business in semiconductor materials and electronic chemicals.

These directions indicate that Formosa Plastics is striving to transform from a "petrochemical company" into a "materials science company." With its deep foundation in chemical engineering and manufacturing experience, the group has the potential to find growth opportunities in emerging fields.

Formosa Plastics is redefining "the social responsibility of petrochemical enterprises." Moving from the pursuit of profit maximization toward simultaneously undertaking environmental and social responsibilities, the group's transition path will serve as a reference case for Taiwan's traditional manufacturing sector.

The story of Formosa Plastics Group is a microcosm of Taiwan's industrialization. It has witnessed Taiwan's journey from poverty to prosperity, and from an agrarian society to an industrial one. In this new era, whether Formosa Plastics can successfully transform will determine the competitiveness of traditional manufacturing in the tide of sustainable development. This is not just a challenge for one company, but a critical indicator for the transformation of Taiwan's entire industry.
