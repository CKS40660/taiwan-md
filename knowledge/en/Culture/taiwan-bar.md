> **30-Second Overview:**
> Taiwan Bar is the pioneer of "knowledge translation" content in Taiwan. Founded in 2014 by Hsieh Cheng-hao (DJ Hauer), Hsiao Yu-chen, and others, its debut work _Animated History of Taiwan_ achieved one million views within a month, successfully transforming dry history into popular IP. However, behind the high-quality content lay heavy financial pressure; the company once faced debts of 20 million TWD. This decade-long experiment ultimately facilitated a strategic transformation from an "audiovisual production company" to a "digital education brand," attempting to become a lighthouse for digital education in Asia through _Beelu's Taiwan Adventures_ and "Great Catch Academy."

## The Opening of History: The Passion and Anxiety Behind That "Deng"

On September 1, 2014, in an office in Taipei, four young people—with an average age of under 30—watched a YouTube upload progress bar with bated breath. It was an animated video titled "Making Big Money Selling Opium? Opium Policy During the Japanese Colonial Period." When the iconic "deng" sound effect played at the end of the video, they could not have predicted that this Formosan black bear named _Beelu_ would sweep across Taiwan within the following month, creating a miracle of over one million views [^1].

The founding team at the time—Hsieh Cheng-hao (DJ Hauer), Hsiao Yu-chen, Chang Chia-chia, and Lin Chen—each brought expertise in music production, history teaching, visual design, and video creation. They were attempting to answer a core question: **"Why are Taiwanese people so indifferent toward their own history?"** Their answer was: **Because we lack a medium that "makes people want to keep watching."**

## Deep Analysis of the IP Universe: The "Beelu Organization" and Cultural Metaphor of Taiwan's Native Species

Taiwan Bar's success lies not only in its narrative but also in its precise character metaphors. Hsiao Yu-chen noted that Taiwan Bar follows a reverse model, starting from "content" and then moving into "character management" [^2]. They established an IP system collectively known as the "Beelu Organization," imbuating Taiwan's native species with distinct contemporary personalities.

### Character Universe and Symbolic Framework

| Character Name     | Prototype Animal         | Cultural Function and Traits                                                                                                           |
| :----------------- | :----------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Beelu (黑啤)**   | Taiwan Black Bear        | **History and Irony**. A lover of alcohol and a trivia fanatic; the "straight man" who breaks down textbook authority through sarcasm. |
| **Braindy (藍地)** | Formosan Sika Deer       | **Science and Logic**. A steady observer, often providing neutral perspectives on controversial issues.                                |
| **Rose (紅瑰)**    | Formosan Land Crocodile  | **Aesthetics and Sensibility**. Responsible for art and sex education series; a character designed with high empathy.                  |
| **Shao (黃紹)**    | Formosan Clouded Leopard | **Economics and Alertness**. Represents survival competition and business logic; frequently appears in economics series.               |
| **Lizi (栗子)**    | Taiwan Horseshoe Bat     | **Childhood and Exploration**. Designed specifically for ages 3-8, representing curiosity and the future.                              |

These characters are not merely for merchandise; they carry profound social concerns. For example, Beelu's backstory mentions that "his family was once invaded by dark forces," which is actually a metaphor for the plight of Taiwan's endangered species [^2].

📝 **Curator's Note:** Taiwan Bar's IP management is an experiment in "semantic symbiosis": characters are containers, holding heavy knowledge that would otherwise be filtered out by the general public.

## The Founder's Divergent Strategy: The Complementary Evolution from "Taiwan Bar" to "STR Network"

In the growth trajectory of Taiwan Bar, 2018 served as a critical turning point. Founders Hsieh Cheng-hao (DJ Hauer) and Hsiao Yu-chen implemented a strategic divergence in their content tracks.

In 2018, Hsieh Cheng-hao founded "STR Network," channeling his passion for comedy and political satire into phenomenon-level programs such as _Boen Night Show_ and _Yan Shang_ [^4]. His logic was "traffic first," attempting to push the boundaries of free speech in Taiwan through an aggressive commercial model.

Meanwhile, Hsiao Yu-chen chose to take over as CEO of Taiwan Bar, pivoting toward "deep educational cultivation." This divergence was not due to a clash of ideologies, but rather an optimization of resource allocation: **Hsieh Cheng-hao focused on expanding the breadth of the entertainment market, while Hsiao Yu-chen focused on consolidating the depth of educational content.**

```tw-versus

Key works: *Boen Night Show*, *Yan Shang* | Key works: *Animated History of Taiwan*, *Little Black Beer Plays Taiwan*
```

## The Lesson of 20 Million TWD Debt: A Perilous Arc from Video Production to Educational Transformation

Following its viral success, Taiwan Bar faced a brutal financial reality. The production cost for high-quality animation can reach tens of thousands of TWD per minute, yet YouTube ad revenue covered less than 10% of expenses. Hsieh Cheng-hao once stated bluntly: "When you're young, you have nothing to lose; besides not sleeping, we had nothing" [^5].

Around 2019, Taiwan Bar's debt once reached as high as 20 million TWD. This crisis prompted Taiwan Bar's most important transformation: **from a "production company with internet presence" to an "educational brand deeply rooted in digital learning."**

### Great Catch Academy: Redefining Literacy Education

Taiwan Bar established "Great Catch Academy" (大抓周學院), shifting its focus from click rates to "student value." They no longer work in isolation but collaborate with various government agencies (such as the Human Rights Museum and the Ocean Affairs Council) to deeply integrate content with the 108 Curriculum [^14].

## Challenges in Global Vision: From "Taiwan-Centric" to "Asia-Centric"

In 2022, Taiwan Bar released the all-English documentary _That’s So Asian_, with production costs per episode reaching 3 million TWD [^14]. In this project, Hsiao Yu-chen proposed a counter-intuitive viewpoint: **"We should not always think from a 'Taiwan-centric' perspective; instead, we should consider what Taiwan can bring to Asia as a member of Asia"** [^14].

This project marked Taiwan Bar's vision moving from local history toward a larger international dialogue, attempting to find Taiwan's position within shared Asian issues such as cram school culture and anime trends.

## Social Impact and Controversy: Tugging Between Fun and Rigor

While Taiwan Bar has won the Golden Pin Design Award and YouTube Creator awards, it has always been subject to scrutiny from academia.

1. **Controversy over Historical Perspectives**: Early works were criticized for being too lighthearted when handling issues regarding "comfort women" and "Indigenous peoples." In response, Taiwan Bar significantly strengthened its advisory board for _New Animated History of Taiwan_ and proactively corrected erroneous content [^8].
2. **The Pain of Commercialization**: In 2024, moving older YouTube works behind a paywall caused backlash from some supporters, but it also established an industry discipline that "high-quality content must be paid for" [^7].

## Conclusion: The Digital Lighthouse for the Next Decade

In 2024, Taiwan Bar celebrated its tenth anniversary. From that initial "Wait for it" to a digital academy that has now accumulated over 15,000 students, Taiwan Bar's trajectory proves: **In the era of fast food, deep content still has room to survive, provided it possesses the courage to evolve.**

They are not just telling the stories of Taiwan; they are building a knowledge waterway toward the future for Taiwan's next generation.
