> **30-Second Overview:** In 1973, Lin Hwai-min, a 26-year-old novelist with no professional dance background, founded "Cloud Gate Dance Theatre," Taiwan's first professional modern dance company. Not only did this troupe become the first contemporary dance company in the Chinese-speaking world, but it has also been active on the international stage for over 50 years, described by _The New York Times_ as "Asia's most important contemporary dance company."

In 1970, 23-year-old Lin Hwai-min was studying creative writing at the University of Iowa's "Writer's Workshop" in the United States. That year, he had already published his breakthrough work _Cicada_ (蟬), making him a prominent young writer in Taiwan's literary circles. But one night in a foreign land, he walked into a dance studio, beginning an adventure that would fundamentally transform the performing arts in Taiwan.

No one could have imagined that three years later, this novelist would establish the first professional modern dance company in the Chinese-speaking world and sustain it for over half a century.

## From Literature to the Body: An Unexpected Turn

Lin Hwai-min was born in 1947 into a distinguished family in Xingang, Chiayi. His great-grandfather was a scholar of the late Qing Dynasty, his grandfather was a renowned physician who studied in Japan, and his father, Lin Chin-sheng, was the first magistrate of Chiayi County. In such a scholarly household, it seemed only natural for Lin to follow a literary path—he began publishing novels at age 14 and published _Cicada_ at 22. The work captured the youthful confusion of Taiwanese youth in the 1960s at the Star Coffee House in Ximending, marking an era.

> **💡 Did You Know?**
> Lin Hwai-min fell in love with dance after watching the film _Red Lotus_ (紅菱艷) at age five, but he did not formally begin training until he was 23.

However, the turn Lin took as a literary youth in Iowa opened an entirely new door for Taiwan. In 1970, he began formal dance training while simultaneously pursuing his degree and studying modern dance. After returning to Taiwan with a Master of Arts in 1972, he made an "impulsive" decision: to found a modern dance company.

It was 1973. There were no government subsidies, no professional venues, and not even enough dancers. Lin Hwai-min and several like-minded young people began Cloud Gate's very first performance in an experimental theater.

## Cloud Gate: A Modern Revolution with an Ancient Name

"Cloud Gate" (雲門) refers to a legendary ritual dance from the era of the Yellow Emperor. Lin's choice of this ancient name was no accident. He sought to create choreography rooted in his own culture rather than mere imitations of Western modern dance. More importantly, he told the media that Cloud Gate's ultimate goal was "to dance for all people."

In 1973, this sounded like an impossible mission. Modern dance was a near-blank slate in Taiwan—where were the audiences? Where was the funding? Yet Lin Hwai-min and his Cloud Gate turned the impossible into reality, step by step.

In 1978, Lin took his dancers to the banks of the Xindian River to carry stones, experiencing the physical movements of ancestral laborers, to choreograph _Tales of the Water Margin_ (薪傳)—an epic dance drama depicting the history of "wood boats crossing Wu River, Tangshan migrants crossing to Taiwan." It was the first theatrical work themed around Taiwanese history.

> **📝 Curator's Note**
> The premiere of _Tales of the Water Margin_ was on December 16, 1978, which happened to be the same day the United States announced it would sever diplomatic ties with the Republic of China. Over the course of two months, this dance drama, which depicted ancestors crossing treacherous waters and struggling to colonize the land, comforted and inspired over 20,000 spectators.

This was not a coincidence; it was a historical resonance. At a moment when Taiwanese society most needed cultural identity, Cloud Gate used the body to tell the story of this land.

## A Modern Language for the Eastern Body

What is most admirable about Lin Hwai-min is his deep excavation and modern transformation of Eastern culture. _Nine Songs_ (九歌, 1993) was adapted from the eponymous poem by Qu Yuan, translating the artistic conception of classical literature into a dynamic aesthetic of dance. _Song of the Wanderer_ (流浪者之歌, 1994) drew inspiration from Hermann Hesse's novels and Buddhist scriptures; in 1994, Lin personally went on a pilgrimage to Bodh Gaya, where the Buddha attained enlightenment. Upon his return, he choreographed this work, which transforms Asian religious rituals into contemporary theatrical ceremonies.

_Ink_ (松煙, 2003) was inspired by Song Dynasty landscape paintings, with dancers moving like ink droplets through swirling mist. _Rice_ (稻禾, 2013) brought the life cycle of the Chishang rice paddies to the stage; in 2013, it was even performed in the actual rice fields of Chishang, Taitung, allowing the dance to merge with the land.

These works embody Lin's aesthetic philosophy of "the body as landscape." He believes that the Eastern body contains cultural codes distinct from the West, and that modern dance should draw nourishment from its own cultural soil.

_The New York Times_ dance review noted: "Lin Hwai-min has successfully fused East and West in terms of dance technique and theatrical concepts."

## Fire and Rebirth: The Calamity of 2008

At 1:00 AM on February 11, 2008—the early morning of the fifth day of the Lunar New Year—a single phone call shattered Lin Hwai-min's peace. Cloud Gate's rehearsal studio in Bali, a corrugated iron structure used for 16 years, was completely destroyed by a massive fire.

"The person who called was a woman living near the rehearsal studio; she happened to be awake. I didn't know her, and I didn't know how she had my number... I rushed there immediately. It was terrifying," Lin recalled afterward.

Performance props, years of archives, costumes—almost everything was lost to the flames. For a company that had existed for 35 years, this was equivalent to having its collective memory burned away.

However, this disaster instead galvanized unprecedented support from Taiwanese society. With help from various sectors, Cloud Gate raised over NT$370 million in reconstruction funds. Three years later, Lin said at a symposium: "Because a fire burned down the old rehearsal studio, Cloud Gate can now look forward to the challenges and happiness of the next 50 years."

In 2011, the Cloud Gate Theater was completed in Tamsui, becoming Taiwan's first theater built through private fundraising.

## Going Global, Staying Local

Under Lin Hwai-min's leadership, starting with its first European tour in 1983, Cloud Gate has traveled to all five continents, with over 300 overseas performances. In 2018, Cloud Gate was awarded the Outstanding Dance Company award at the UK National Dance Awards.

Yet Lin has never allowed international success to make him forget his local responsibilities. Every year, Cloud Gate conducts outdoor performances across Taiwan—from Taipei's Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall to remote townships—giving more citizens the chance to experience modern dance. Lin once said: "If you take away the outdoor performances, Cloud Gate would immediately fall ill and go to the hospital."

> **⚠️ Controversial Viewpoint**
> While Cloud Gate's outdoor performances are widely popular, some critics have questioned whether this "audience-pleasing" approach affects artistic purity. Lin's response was: "I am a choreographer trained by outdoor audiences."

## International Recognition and Lifetime Achievement

In 2013, Lin Hwai-min received the Lifetime Achievement Award at the American Dance Festival, an honor often referred to as the "Nobel Prize of Modern Dance." He was the fifth person to receive it after Martha Graham, Merce Cunningham, Pina Bausch, and William Forsythe, and the first Asian choreographer to receive this distinction.

Additionally, he has received other major international awards, including the Lifetime Achievement Award at the Germany Tanz im August International Dance Festival (2009), the Rockefeller III Award in the US, and the Chevalier de l'Ordre des Arts et des Lettres from France.

But for Lin Hwai-min, his greatest achievement may not be these awards, but the complete ecosystem Cloud Gate has built: from Cloud Gate Dance Theatre and Cloud Gate 2 to the Cloud Gate Classrooms, he has created a comprehensive system that allowed modern dance to take root in Taiwan.

## Succession: From Lin Hwai-min to Cheng Tsung-lung

In 2017, a car accident caused the 70-year-old Lin Hwai-min to begin thinking about succession. At the end of 2019, he officially retired, handing the reins of Cloud Gate to Cheng Tsung-lung (鄭宗龍), who had previously served as the artistic director of Cloud Gate 2.

"If I don't retire, he will turn into Prince Charles," Lin joked.

This succession process took many years; Lin deliberately cultivated a successor to ensure that Cloud Gate's artistic spirit could continue. In 2020, Cheng Tsung-lung officially assumed the role of Artistic Director of Cloud Gate Dance Theatre, presenting his first work after taking over, _Dingguang_ (定光).

Lin once said: "I hope Cloud Gate is a brand, not a one-man company." Under his 46 years of leadership, Cloud Gate has choreographed a total of 90 works and trained countless dancers, many of whom have become significant dancers and choreographers themselves, creating the influence known as the "Cloud Gate Style."

## The End of an Era and a New Beginning

In 2000, Lin Hwai-min received the National Cultural Award. In 2019, a 50th-anniversary edition of his novel _Cicada_ was released. In 2022, his book _Surge and Reflection_ (激流與倒影) won both the Golden Award of the Taiwan Literature Awards and the first prize for Non-fiction at the Taipei International Book Awards.

From novelist to dancer, from an individual's impulse to a symbol of an era, Lin Hwai-min has used half a century to prove one thing: a true artist is not an imitator, but a creator. He brought modern dance to Taiwan from nothing, moving it from the niche to the mainstream, and from local roots to international stages.

> **✦** "In a person's life, everything can be taken away, except for dreams." — Lin Hwai-min

Cloud Gate's 50 years is a story of pursuing dreams, and a microcosm of Taiwan's culture growing from nothing to something, from weak to strong. On the Cloud Gate stage, what moves is the very soul of this island.
